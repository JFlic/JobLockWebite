Privacy Policy

Last updated 13 September 2026

Your job log stays on your iPhone.

There is no account and no server, and JobLock's own code sends nothing anywhere. Every job application you log, every answer you give during onboarding, and every setting you choose is written to storage on your iPhone and stays there. Delete the app and it is all gone.

The one exception is not ours: the Google text-recognition software that reads your screenshots sends Google its own usage and diagnostic data. Exactly what, below.

The rest of this page spells out exactly what is stored, what leaves the phone, which permissions the app asks for, and why — because "we respect your privacy" is easy to write and hard to check.

What JobLock stores on your device

All of this lives in a database inside JobLock's own storage on your phone. None of it is transmitted.

What JobLock does not do

  • It does not keep your screenshots. A confirmation screenshot is read once — to check it really shows a submitted application and to fingerprint it — and then discarded. No copy is saved.
  • It does not keep your signature. The commitment you sign at the end of onboarding is drawn on screen and thrown away when you move on. It is never saved.
  • It has no servers. There is nowhere for your data to be uploaded to, and JobLock's own code has nothing that could upload it.
  • It has no account. There is no sign-up, email address or password. The name you give during onboarding stays on your phone.
  • It adds no analytics, crash reporting or advertising of its own, and it does not track you across other apps or websites.
  • It does not sell or share your information.

What Google's text recognition sends

JobLock reads your confirmation screenshots with Google ML Kit Text Recognition. The recognition runs on your phone: the model is bundled inside the app, and your screenshots are read there, not uploaded.

ML Kit does, however, send Google information about the software itself. In the privacy manifest Google ships with ML Kit, Google declares that it collects:

  • a device identifier
  • performance data and other diagnostic data
  • product interaction — how the feature is used
  • "other user content" and "other data types", which Google does not describe further

Google states this data is used for analytics and to make the feature work, is not linked to your identity, and is not used for tracking. JobLock cannot see what is sent and receives none of it. Google's handling of it is covered by Google's Privacy Policy.

Why this matters for the App Store label: because of ML Kit, JobLock's privacy label on the App Store is not "Data Not Collected". It lists the data above as data not linked to you. An earlier version of this page said the label read "Data Not Collected"; that did not account for ML Kit, and it was wrong.

Permissions, and what each one is really for

Photos

When a session is running, JobLock looks for the confirmation screenshot you just took, reads the text out of it on your device, and — if it counts — removes that screenshot from your camera roll for you. Deleted photos go to Recently Deleted, where iOS keeps them for 30 days, so nothing is irreversible.

Being straight with you: iOS grants photo access to your whole library, not to a slice of it. JobLock only ever looks at images created after you started a session, and only deletes ones it logged for you. That restriction is the app's own rule, not something iOS enforces — so you are taking JobLock's word for it. Nothing it reads leaves your phone either way.

Camera

Only used if you choose to photograph a confirmation shown on another screen — a laptop, say. The photo is read on your device and is not saved to your camera roll or anywhere else.

Notifications

Optional — you can say "Not now" during onboarding and the app works the same. If you allow them, JobLock schedules reminders on your phone itself. They are local notifications: no push server is involved, and nothing is sent to deliver them.

Screen Time

JobLock uses Apple's Screen Time framework to shield the apps you pick while you are applying. Apple deliberately hands the app opaque tokens rather than app names: JobLock can raise a shield over your selection and count how many things are in it, but it is never told which apps you chose. That selection is stored on your device and is never transmitted.

Other software inside JobLock

  • Google ML Kit Text Recognition reads the text in your screenshots on your device. It sends Google its own usage and diagnostic data — see What Google's text recognition sends.
  • Apple Screen Time (Family Controls and Managed Settings) provides the app shielding described above.
  • Apple's rating prompt. If you tap "Rate JobLock", iOS shows its own rating sheet. Apple handles it end to end; JobLock is never told whether you rated it or what you said.
  • Links out. Tapping "Go to job posting" opens your browser or the relevant app. Once you leave JobLock, that site's privacy policy applies, not this one.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes everything JobLock stored — the log, your onboarding answers, your settings, and your shielded-app selection — in one step. JobLock never sent any of it anywhere, so no copy of it survives somewhere you cannot reach.

There is no "request my data" process, because JobLock holds no copy of your data to send you. It is on your phone, and you already have it. The diagnostic data ML Kit sends goes to Google, not to JobLock, and is not linked to you.

Children

JobLock is not directed at children under 13 and is not designed for them. JobLock itself sends no personal information anywhere, from anyone of any age; the Google diagnostic data described above is sent the same way for every user.

Changes to this policy

If the app's behaviour changes in a way that affects your privacy, this page gets updated and the date at the top changes with it. Material changes will also be called out in the app's release notes rather than slipped in quietly.
