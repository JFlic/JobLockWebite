Shielding is a commitment device, not a lock

JobLock uses Apple's Screen Time framework to put a shield over apps you select. Being straight about what that is worth:

  • You can end a session, change your selection, or delete JobLock at any time, and your apps come back. That is by design — it is your phone.
  • iOS controls the shield, not us. Apple can change how the framework behaves in any update, and shields may fail, lift late, or behave differently across iOS versions and device states.
  • JobLock cannot see which apps you picked. Apple hands the app opaque tokens rather than app names, so we cannot verify, report on, or troubleshoot your specific selection.

Do not rely on JobLock to block anything that actually matters. It is designed to add friction to a habit you already want to break — not to enforce abstinence, supervise a person, or serve as a safety mechanism. If you need something you cannot undo, JobLock is the wrong tool.

If you keep screenshots you cannot afford to lose, do not start a session with them undeleted in your library. What the app does with the contents of those images is covered in the Privacy Policy.
